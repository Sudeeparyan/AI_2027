7.5	Specific module resources required (if applicable)
Learners will use a high-level programming language such as Python for the implementation and experimentation of generative AI models. Development and execution environments may include Anaconda, Visual Studio Code (VS Code), Jupyter Notebook, Google Colab and Cloud based Platforms to support interactive coding, experimentation, and prototyping.

Students will install and work with a range of machine learning and deep learning libraries, including but not limited to PyTorch, TensorFlow, and Hugging Face Transformers and Diffusers, for building, fine-tuning, and evaluating generative models. Additional software tools may be used such as Git and Docker for the deployment of generative AI applications.

7.6	Application of programme teaching, learning and assessment strategies to this module
Teaching & Learning:
The teaching and learning strategy for the Generative AI module comprises a combination of lectures, tutorials, lab exercises, case studies, group discussions and independent learning activities. Each week, students will participate in a 2-hour lecture and a 2-hour tutorial/lab session.
The practical tutorial and lab sessions are designed to enable students to apply theoretical concepts to real-world generative AI problems, thereby reinforcing learning through hands-on experience. 
All module materials, including lecture content, lab instructions, supplementary resources, and assessment information, are made available to students through the Moodle learning management system.
The module supports both face-to-face and blended delivery modes. NCI’s blended learning model combines in-person classes with asynchronous, directed learning activities, such as pre-recorded videos, guided exercises, and online quizzes, which students are expected to complete in advance of live sessions. This approach promotes active learning and ensures effective use of classroom time.
Assessment:
Assessment in this module is by a Group Project (60%) and a terminal exam (40%). 
Group Project: Students will work in groups of two to three to design, implement, and evaluate a generative AI application addressing a real-world problem in a specific domain. The project will require students to apply theoretical concepts and practical skills learned throughout the module, such as generative model architectures, prompt engineering, fine-tuning, multimodal learning, comparative analysis of different models, evaluation and responsible AI practices.
Exam: The examination will be of two hours duration and may include a mix of: theoretical, applied and interpretation questions.
Re-assessment:
Re-assessment of this module will consist of a new submission of an individual project that demonstrates achievement of the minimum intended learning outcomes. All learning outcomes will be reassessed in the repeat attempt, and students will be afforded opportunities to resubmit at specified times throughout the academic year.
