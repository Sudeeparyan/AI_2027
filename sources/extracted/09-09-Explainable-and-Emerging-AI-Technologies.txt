7.6	Application of programme teaching, learning and assessment strategies to this module
Teaching & Learning:
The learning strategy combines lectures, demonstrations, and practical explainable and emerging AI technologies to reinforce conceptual understanding through application. The module adopts a learner-centred approach, encouraging students to take ownership of their practical work while receiving structured guidance from instructors. Sessions are interactive and project-driven, providing opportunities for learning by doing, collaborative problem-solving, and applied experimentation in XAI.
Assessment:
Assessment in this module is by continuous assessment (50%) and project (50%).
The continuous assessment will typically consist of hands-on labs and mini-projects where students critically apply concepts of explainable AI, system-level XAI, safety, human-centred oversight, sustainability, and compliance. Students will produce micro deliverables such as model cards, counterfactual explanation tables, SHAP/LIME visualisations, audit logs, drift monitoring reports, and energy usage charts, demonstrating both technical accuracy and responsible AI practices.
The project component will require students to design, implement, and evaluate a system-level Trustworthy AI assurance plan for a real or simulated AI application. Deliverables include a technical report, supporting artefacts (e.g., lineage logs, drift metrics, energy measurements, safety audits), and a presentation or demonstration of the operational pipeline. This structure ensures that students can understand core principles, critically evaluate AI systems, and apply them responsibly in realistic deployment scenarios.
Re-assessment:
Reassessment of this module will consist of an individual project re-submission that assesses all module learning outcomes. Students will be given an opportunity to complete the reassessment project at specified times during the academic year, demonstrating their understanding and practical application of scalable analytics and cloud computing concepts.
