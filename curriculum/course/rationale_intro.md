# Purpose and scope

This document explains the proposed revision of **Section 7.3, Indicative Module Content, Organisation and Structure**, of the Generative AI module descriptor. It accompanies two copies of the descriptor in the same folder:

* **Descriptor - Generative AI (7.3 revised).docx**: a clean copy with the proposed text.
* **Descriptor - Generative AI (7.3 tracked changes).docx**: the same edit shown as Word tracked changes, so each change can be accepted or rejected individually.

**What is unchanged.** Every other section of the descriptor is byte-for-byte identical to the original template, including the module overview, MIMLOs (7.2), learning and assessment strategy (7.6, 7.7) and reading lists (7.9); this was verified automatically by comparing every part of the Word file. Within Section 7.3, the **twelve lecture topics and their order are unchanged**. Only the **Detail** and **Tutorials (Examples)** columns are revised.

# Principles of the revision

1. **Keep the professor's structure; make the content specific and current.** Several original cells list headings only (for example "Theoretical Foundations. Core Architecture. Variants."). The revision names the concepts behind each heading, as they are taught in 2026, so that the descriptor is informative for students, external examiners and validation panels.
2. **A general overview of all important concepts.** Other modules in the programme cover machine learning and deep learning in depth, so this module surveys every major family of generative models and the main ways of using them (prompting, fine-tuning, multimodal models, deployment, RAG and agents), without going deeper than an MSc overview requires.
3. **How to use AI tools.** The module brief includes using AI tools well. This is now explicit in week 1 (the tool landscape and responsible use), week 7 (prompting with evaluation), weeks 11–12 (APIs, deployment, RAG and agents).
4. **Evaluation and responsible AI in every week.** MIMLO 4 (critical evaluation, fairness, bias, hallucination) is practised throughout rather than in a single week: each Detail cell names the relevant evaluation methods, and risks such as deepfakes, prompt injection and data protection appear where they arise.
5. **Model families, not versions.** Names such as GPT, Gemini, Claude, Llama, Qwen, Mistral and DeepSeek are used without version numbers, so the text stays valid as versions change every few months.
6. **Practical tutorials on free infrastructure.** Tutorials target Google Colab with open models and CPU alternatives, in line with Section 7.5 (Python, Jupyter, Colab, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers and Diffusers, Git and Docker). Free GPU availability and memory vary; the instructor guide gives preparation options. Local checks do not verify a live Colab session.
7. **Current regulation.** Article 50 transparency duties are reflected in weeks 1, 10 and 11, with provider/deployer roles and exceptions. They apply from 2 August 2026; Article 111(4) gives providers of systems placed on the market before that date until 2 December 2026 to meet Article 50(2)'s marking duty.

# Alignment with MIMLOs and assessment

The revision does not change the MIMLOs or the assessment strategy (group project 60%, proctored examination 40%). It strengthens their coverage: MIMLO 1 (core concepts and mathematics) through the named foundations in weeks 1–6; MIMLO 2 (multimodal) in weeks 4, 9 and 10; MIMLO 3 (implement and fine-tune with parameter-efficient methods) through the tutorials in weeks 2–5, 8 and 10–12; MIMLO 4 (evaluation and ethics) in every week; and MIMLO 5 (deployment, retrieval-augmented and agentic systems) in weeks 11–12, prepared by weeks 6–7.
